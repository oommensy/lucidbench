# LucidBench Annotation Rubric

Each dimension should be annotated independently. The core scientific idea is that **lucidity is not equivalent to dream control**.

Use a 0–2 scale unless otherwise specified:

- **0:** no evidence
- **1:** plausible/implicit evidence
- **2:** explicit/unambiguous evidence

## 1. Awareness

Does the dreamer know, while the dream is occurring, that the current experience is a dream?

**2:** “I realized I was dreaming.”
**1:** performs a reality check with apparent suspicion but no explicit conclusion.
**0:** retrospective statement only, such as “It was a strange dream.”

## 2. Agency

Does the dreamer intentionally select actions rather than merely react?

**2:** explicit intention/decision followed by an action.
**1:** goal-directed behavior is implied.
**0:** dream events simply happen to the dreamer.

## 3. Dream control

Does the dreamer intentionally alter dream content, physics, characters, location, or sensory experience?

**2:** deliberate and successful alteration.
**1:** attempted but uncertain/unsuccessful control.
**0:** no deliberate alteration.

## 4. Metacognition

Does the dreamer reason about their own mental state, the reliability of perception, or the implications of being in a dream?

**2:** explicit reflective reasoning.
**1:** brief questioning or reality testing.
**0:** no metacognitive evidence.

## 5. Waking-memory access

Does the dreamer recall waking-life goals, facts, experiments, relationships, or planned actions while still dreaming?

**2:** explicit waking-memory retrieval influencing dream behavior.
**1:** probable waking reference without clear functional use.
**0:** none.

## 6. Stability / instability

Does lucidity or deliberate control affect the continuation/stability of the dream?

- **2:** explicit instability or explicit stabilization attempt.
- **1:** possible fading/transition linked to awareness.
- **0:** none mentioned.

## Annotation notes

- Do not infer lucidity simply from impossible dream events.
- Do not infer control from flying unless the report says or strongly implies it was intentional.
- Retrospective narration must not be mistaken for in-dream awareness.
- Annotators should label evidence in the report, not what they believe “must have happened.”
